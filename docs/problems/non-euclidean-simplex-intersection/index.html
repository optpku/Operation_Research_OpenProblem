Simplex–Ball Intersection in Spherical and Hyperbolic Space

Proposed by: Gábor Fejes Tóth and Yinyu Ye

In spherical or hyperbolic space, does a concentric regular simplex maximize intersection with a fixed ball among simplices of a fixed volume?

Statement

Use geodesic volume in spherical space $S^d$ or hyperbolic space $H^d$. Fix a geodesic ball $B$ and an admissible simplex volume $V$. For every nondegenerate geodesic $d$-simplex $T$ of volume $V$, is

$$\mathrm{vol}_d(B\cap T)\le\mathrm{vol}_d(B\cap T_{\rm reg}),$$

where $T_{\rm reg}$ is a regular simplex of volume $V$ concentric with $B$?

In the spherical case, the hemisphere and convexity conventions, ball radius, admissible volumes, and existence of the comparison simplex remain to be fixed.

Background

Euclidean symmetrization favors regular simplices in several volume problems. Curvature changes this argument, while fixing the simplex volume and maximizing its intersection with a ball also requires control of relative position.

Known results

The problem chapter [1] records the Euclidean analogue. Böröczky [2] and Peyerimhoff [3] study maximal-volume simplices in spherical or hyperbolic balls. Those inscribed-volume results have different constraints and do not directly establish the fixed-volume intersection inequality.
